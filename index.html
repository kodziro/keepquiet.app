Frequently asked questions

About the free online noise meter

Is the online noise meter free?

Yes, the online noise meter at keepquiet.app is free: no sign-up, no ads and no time limit. The free set is one character (a cat) and one scene. An optional one-time unlock for $19.99 plus tax where applicable adds all 8 characters and 5 backgrounds; the free meter keeps working without it.

Does it record audio?

No. Audio never leaves your device — sound is analysed in the browser, nothing is recorded, stored or sent. We count anonymous page views and clicks (TelemetryDeck, no cookies), never sound. Pressing pause switches the microphone off.

Which browsers and devices does it work on?

It works on Chromebook, Windows, Mac and iPad in a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox) and needs a microphone. The page must be opened over https (keepquiet.app is), and the browser will ask once for microphone permission. On a managed school Chromebook the microphone can be blocked by policy; the IT admin can allow it for keepquiet.app.

Why does my smart board pick the wrong microphone?

Interactive boards often have several microphones (the board itself, a webcam, the connected laptop) and the browser starts with the default one. Choose the right one in the Microphone list under the meter; the choice is remembered on that device. If the meter does not react, try another entry in the list or check that the microphone is not muted in the system settings.

Can I get it as an app?

Yes. Noise Meter – Keep Quiet is the same noise meter as an app for iPhone, iPad and Mac, free to download with no ads. The app is installed on your device and works offline, with optional premium characters and themes via in-app purchase. There is no Android app; on Android, Chromebook and Windows use the free online noise meter. Get it on the App Store →

No. There is no account at keepquiet.app. Stripe asks for your email address at checkout to send the receipt; we use the same address to send your license key and to restore your purchase later.

How do I unlock the other devices I use?

Open your restore link on that device, or type your license key — or the email address you paid with — into “Already unlocked? Restore”. The restore link and the key are shown after payment and sent by email. The unlock is for the devices you use yourself, with no device limit; sharing the key with others is not allowed. The unlock is saved in the browser: Safari may forget it after a week away, and shared or managed computers may clear it at sign-out — then open the restore link again.

I lost my license key — what can I do?

Type the email address you paid with into “Already unlocked? Restore”: if that email paid, we send the restore link and the key again — check your spam folder too. Use the exact address you entered at checkout. No email after 10 minutes? Write to support@keepquiet.app.

About the app

What is a visual noise meter for kids?

A visual noise meter turns sound into something children can see. Keep Quiet uses the device microphone to measure the room's volume in real time: a friendly character changes expression, the scene shifts colour, and a green-to-red dial or bar shows how loud things are — so kids can lower their own volume without being told. It is available as a free online noise meter in the browser and as the Noise Meter – Keep Quiet app for iPhone, iPad and Mac. Learn more →

How do I monitor noise levels in my classroom?

Open the free online noise meter on the classroom screen, or the app on an iPad mirrored to the whiteboard, pick a character, and let the class watch. As chatter rises the character reacts and the meter climbs toward red, prompting students to self-correct — no shushing needed. Learn more →

How can I get kids to be quiet without yelling?

Replace verbal reminders with a visual cue. When children can see the noise level — and a character reacting to it — staying quiet becomes a game of keeping the character happy rather than obeying an instruction. Learn more →

Does the app have a quiet time timer?

Yes, the app has a quiet-time timer. Set a quiet-time goal — say, ten minutes of reading — and the timer runs while the meter shows the live noise level, turning quiet time into a challenge kids can win. The free online noise meter has a quiet-time timer too: choose a preset from 1 to 30 minutes or type your own length from 1 to 90 minutes. It counts only while the meter runs; at 0:00 the meter pauses itself and the timer turns red, with no sound. Learn more →

Is it suitable for autistic children and sensory-friendly spaces?

Yes. Gentle visuals, colour feedback that never punishes, reduced-motion and high-contrast accessibility support, dark interface, and no ads or interruptions make it a good fit for sensory-friendly rooms. The online noise meter respects the reduced-motion setting of the device too. Learn more →

How loud is too loud for a classroom?

Focused work is comfortable around normal-conversation levels or lower; sustained loud noise makes concentration and reading harder. Every room differs, so the meter shows the level at a glance and lets you set the sensitivity so that "too loud" matches each activity. Learn more →
